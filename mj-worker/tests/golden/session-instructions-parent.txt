Commit and call `request_review` before `git push` or opening a pull or merge request. Plain documentation-only pushes that the pre-push hook exempts may skip review; scripts, configuration, lockfiles, and MDX still need review. A documentation commit on top of unreviewed code does not qualify. A conflict-free merge of upstream history into cleanly reviewed work, with no resolution or other edit, also skips review; conflict resolutions, extra edits, and merges onto an override still need review unless `set_baseline` first marks the override. The tool replies immediately; the verdict arrives later as a Mjolnir message. With no `paths` it reviews the repository of your working directory; pass `paths` to review other worktrees, or several together in one review. Reviews of different worktrees run at the same time; a worktree is in one open review at a time, and a request that overlaps one is refused with what to do next. Reviewing a worktree at HEAD lets you keep working meanwhile. When multiple reviewers run, partial results arrive as each finishes: start investigating and fixing findings you agree with as soon as they arrive instead of idling until all reviewers finish, while waiting for the final verdict before committing, calling `override_review`, requesting another review, or pushing. A push is allowed when the hook exempts the change, after every reviewer is clean, or after `override_review` on the reviewed commits. Retry incomplete reviews. Do not fix every finding by default: when a verdict arrives, call `override_review` first, before committing anything, with each finding you will not fix and your reason (pass `path` when more than one verdict is outstanding); later reviewers are told not to raise those again. Then fix the rest, commit, and request review again. Use `set_baseline` to mark commits through a reviewed commit as done, so later reviews start after it.

- [P1] Preserve uncertainty for qualified Scala visibility — crates/bifrost-jvm/src/scala/resolution.rs:508-509
  For declarations such as `private[pkg]` or `protected[pkg]`, the source visibility only records `Private` or `Protected` and discards the qualifier. Removing the visibility gap therefore makes the new policy definitively deny legal accesses from elsewhere in `pkg`; conversely, `private[this]` is treated as ordinary private and can be allowed from another instance or a companion. The qualifier must be represented in the access decision, or these cases must remain incomplete.
- [P2] Account for companion access to protected members — crates/bifrost-analysis/src/analyzer/store/resolution_operation/java_access.rs:237-241
  When a Scala companion object accesses a protected member of its companion class, its enclosing template is the object, which is not a subtype of the class. The subclass check consequently denies a legal access, although companion pairs are already available to this policy. The same problem occurs in the opposite direction for protected members of an object.
- [P2] Check the receiver of a protected access — crates/bifrost-analysis/src/analyzer/store/resolution_operation/java_access.rs:238-241
  For an access from a subclass through a receiver typed as the base class, the caller is a subtype so this context yields `Allowed` even though Scala's protected access also restricts the qualifying receiver. This can resolve an inaccessible member as a complete result; the receiver's type needs to participate in the decision or the result must remain incomplete.
